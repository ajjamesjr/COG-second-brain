# Data Platform Migration

## What is this project?
Not yet a running platform — it's the "Define" phase of an architecture/BA engagement, delivered by a partner called **Oakland** for Packt, running 7–30 Sep 2026 (10 working days, "sixteen deliverables across five tracks") [Source: packt-data-platform-v2 repo, docs/architecture/deliverables.html | 2026-09-21 | confidence: high]. The repo itself states: *"`platform/` is empty of contracts, models and checks, and that is correct. No requirement has reached `agreed`."* All requirements filed to date are still `status: candidate`; a CI-enforced traceability gate (`tools/traceability.py`) blocks any implementation code from citing a requirement that isn't `agreed` yet — so no platform code exists by design at this stage [Source: packt-data-platform-v2 repo, README.md | 2026-09-21 | confidence: high].

The repo is organized as four concerns: **Method** (`skills/`, `docs/standards/`), **Agreement** (`ba-corpus/`, `docs/adrs/`), **Implementation** (`platform/`, `infrastructure/`, `tests/` — currently empty stubs), **Shared reference** (`registry/`). The `ba-corpus/` is an evidence→requirement pipeline: Evidence (EV) → Findings (FND) → Requirements (REQ), plus Questions (Q) and Decisions (DEC records), each schema-validated with CI-enforced provenance [Source: packt-data-platform-v2 repo, README.md | 2026-09-21 | confidence: high].

As of commit `db776d7c` (2026-09-23), the corpus holds 25 evidence records, 66 findings, 38 requirements, 26 open questions and 6 decisions [Source: packt-data-platform-v2 repo, ba-corpus/ tree at db776d7c | 2026-09-23 | confidence: high].

## Sub-Projects
Two of Arjun's own initiatives are folded in here as sub-projects, because both are threads of this same engagement rather than separate work: their evidence lives inside this BA corpus, and both depend on source systems this platform migration is standardizing.
- [[book-market-database/PROJECT-OVERVIEW|Book Market Database & Competitor Analysis]] — the competitor market-sizing project whose brief (`EV-0006`) is the corpus's primary market-intelligence evidence record.
- [[sp-api-data-program/PROJECT-OVERVIEW|SP-API Data Program]] — the effort to operationalize Packt's existing Amazon Vendor/Seller access, underlying the Keepa/Brand Analytics/SP-API source systems (`SRC-029`, `SRC-031`) both this platform and the Book Market Database depend on.

## Target Architecture (Decided, Not Yet Built)
Six decision records (`ba-corpus/decisions/DEC-0001..0006`), all decided by **Saad Kamal** (ST-004, Packt's data platform lead):
- Databricks on AWS as the single data engine, replacing Redshift/Airflow/dbt/Meltano/Fivetran/Matillion/Hevo entirely — explicitly expected to raise platform spend, traded for a smaller team (DEC-0001, decided 21 Aug 2026) [Source: packt-data-platform-v2 repo, ba-corpus/decisions/DEC-0001 | 2026-08-21 | confidence: high].
- Greenfield rebuild, not lift-and-shift — each legacy source gets onboarded "as a business process," not reproduced as a table (DEC-0002) [Source: packt-data-platform-v2 repo, ba-corpus/decisions/DEC-0002 | 2026-09-21 | confidence: high].
- Infrastructure-as-code, CI/CD, RBAC from day one (DEC-0003); GitHub monorepo (DEC-0004, this repo); Linear for work tracking (DEC-0005); metadata-driven architecture, not pipeline-per-process (DEC-0006) [Source: packt-data-platform-v2 repo, ba-corpus/decisions/DEC-0003..0006 | 2026-09-21 | confidence: high].
- A seventh call sits outside the DEC set on purpose: Databricks becomes Packt's BI surface, retiring the Power BI estate. It isn't filed as a DEC because its own status marker says the sequencing is open — which reports get rebuilt, which get retired, whether an undiscovered dependency exists, and the decommissioning date are all still open, assigned to Packt with Oakland input [Source: packt-data-platform-v2 repo, ba-corpus/decisions/README.md | 2026-09-08 | confidence: high].

**Provenance caveat on all six records**: the `decided_by` (Saad Kamal) and `date` (21 Aug 2026) fields were not sourced from a written statement by Saad Kamal or a Packt-leadership sign-off. Oakland's own principal architect (ST-050) confirmed on 8 Sept 2026, from knowledge held outside the corpus, that Saad Kamal took all six calls; the date convention states the record's filing date stands in for the decision date since no minuted date exists. Both fields were originally drafted by Claude on Oakland's instruction, which the schema explicitly permits for options but not for the outcome itself — a departure the record documents rather than hides. What's still outstanding, separately, is Packt leadership formally backing these six decisions as settled — the exact ask EV-0012's kickoff deck itself makes to "Packt leadership" and has not yet had answered [Source: packt-data-platform-v2 repo, ba-corpus/decisions/README.md | 2026-09-08 | confidence: high].

## CTO Discovery: Target State, Ownership Gaps and Commercial Framing
CTO Alex Major's discovery session (EV-0009, 2026-09-02) is the largest single record in the corpus and the only one held by someone with `can-agree` authority over `platform` — he can commit Packt to something the way no other interviewed stakeholder can. Target architecture: event-driven/API-driven, "microservice-esque," landing at roughly twenty services, with one ERP and one CRM remaining after eighteen months clearing a legacy estate that once ran two non-communicating ERPs synchronizing each other's databases. His own two platform priorities: an MCP interface so LLMs/agents can query the platform read-only ("I don't see rights being something that we do via LLMs" — matches REQ-0006), and democratised bronze-layer ingestion — today only 4 data-team members (2 of them engineers) can add a source, versus 11 developers who could if given a template (REQ-0005) [Source: packt-data-platform-v2 repo, ba-corpus/evidence/EV-0009 | 2026-09-02 | confidence: high].

Business shape, per the CTO: two businesses under one group — verticals/publishing under "Aaron" (nine portfolio directors, later corrected to seven — see Organizational Ambiguities below) and Expert Edge under Ollie/Oliver Huggins (ST-003). Data ownership "doesn't exist" org-wide with one exception — Oliver Huggins over partnership data; product ownership and marketing are named as the two org-wide capability gaps, and weak product ownership is cited as the specific reason the decade-old Amazon integration was never revisited [Source: packt-data-platform-v2 repo, ba-corpus/findings/FND-0008,0011 | 2026-09-02 | confidence: high].

Commercial framing: ~100 channels/subscription partners carry the business, but the top ten cover ~95% of revenue — the CTO would rather drop the long tail than integrate it (FND-0009). Amazon gets named as the platform's first proposed use case (EV-0012 slide 9: Selling Partner API, Keepa, page scraping — see Book Market Database connection below) partly because margin isn't knowable today: Amazon price-matches Packt titles without notice (e.g. RRP 44.99 → 35.99) and runs self-funded ad campaigns, while still paying revenue share calculated on the original price (FND-0005). Cost baseline: ~£5,000/month on Power BI licences "and random bits," ~$10,000 in AWS savings already banked from data-platform optimisation, and uncontrolled query cost across an eventual ~300 self-service users flagged as the real risk to manage once REQ-0004 ships (corroborated independently by EV-0010) [Source: packt-data-platform-v2 repo, ba-corpus/evidence/EV-0009,EV-0010 | 2026-09-02 | confidence: high].

Current-estate scale, from Packt's own pre-engagement statement of direction (EV-0011, authored by Saad Kamal): ~56 declared sources, 158 bespoke Airflow DAGs, Airflow 2.5 on Docker Compose embedding dbt and Meltano as git submodules, Redshift `redclusterdb` in eu-west-1, ~25 Meltano extractors, ~100 Power BI reports (only ~4 in regular use, ~7 occasional) [Source: packt-data-platform-v2 repo, ba-corpus/evidence/EV-0011 | 2026-08-21 | confidence: high].

## Legacy Estate Being Replaced
Documented via `EV-0013`, Data Sources Overview, dated 2 Jul 2026: Redshift (sole warehouse, full-refresh-only), Airflow, dbt, Meltano (unmanaged connectors), Superset (a second, ungoverned BI surface). The source registry grew from 11 to 68 systems once EV-0013 was reconciled — **51 of 68 have no accountable owner**. Four systems land in Redshift via duplicate/parallel ingestion paths; Amazon data alone arrives via three separate channels (SP-API, Brand Analytics, and ParseHub scraping). No single source of truth for customers; only two engineers cover ingestion (stated bottleneck) [Source: packt-data-platform-v2 repo, ba-corpus/findings/FND-0007,0028,0029,0030,0036,0037,0039 | 2026-09-21 | confidence: high].

Further gaps surfaced since: Superset is configured as a second BI layer in the Meltano project feeding off dbt marts, but appears in no diagram or registry file and — unlike Power BI's audited ~4-of-100-reports-in-regular-use — has no usage count at all (FND-0018). Whether Zuora or Chargebee is the live subscription-billing system is directly contested: EV-0013 (2 Jul 2026) shows Zuora still live via two ingestion paths; a data engineer on EV-0003 says Chargebee replaced it "not even a year" ago — both readings survive since one predates the other, and authority for resolving it (Q-0016) now sits with Saad Kamal (FND-0020). A working ML capability already exists in the legacy estate: a Hugging Face inference API is called from a bespoke Airflow DAG to classify Amazon review sentiment/keywords into Redshift (FND-0019). Packt has already built a working prototype of the proposed metadata-driven pipeline framework, but it's one person's work, has no environment isolation, and has never run under load (FND-0038). And the repository can't fully describe the estate it documents: some Airflow DAGs reference services only by connection ID, resolved only in the Airflow metadata database, and Fivetran's connector scope lives only in the Fivetran UI (FND-0040).

## Contracts and Exit Positions
From Saad Kamal's written answer (EV-0010, 2026-09-07), corroborated independently by the CTO (EV-0009): Fivetran is already cancelled, moved to a rolling usage plan that dropped spend from ~$1,700/month to ~$300/month. Power BI runs on ~170 monthly-rolling licences — Saad declined a 12-month renewal in March (worth a 12% saving) because he expected the new platform live by year-end. RudderStack renews annually each September; as of the corpus's filing date the renewal window was still open and time-critical (Q-0006), and RudderStack itself is paid-for but not connected to any warehouse (`SRC-011`, `active-underused`). NetSuite can't be exited until a new royalty service takes over its balances and history: the stated sequence is a parallel royalty run in March 2027, first live run June 2027, notice to NetSuite July 2027, with Sage or QuickBooks as candidate replacements ("a bit chicken and egg"). One uncited, unattributed line in the same evidence claims finance wants to cancel NetSuite in July **2028** instead — directly conflicting with the July 2027 notice date — and is deliberately left open as Q-0005, unresolved as of filing [Source: packt-data-platform-v2 repo, ba-corpus/evidence/EV-0009,EV-0010 | 2026-09-07 | confidence: high].

## Finance and Channel Reconciliation
Three finance knowledge-transfer sessions in September (EV-0021/0022/0023, the first **facilitated by Arjun James**) plus a channel tracker spreadsheet (EV-0020) expose how much of Packt's channel revenue runs on manual labour today. Of 123-124 active sales channels, finance itself rates 27 "Difficult," 69 "Moderate," and only 27 "Easy" — and even an "Easy" channel is a manual download → CSV → verify → upload cycle into both SDNG and NetSuite, with recurring manual steps for ISBN lookup and FX conversion (FND-0048).

Amazon-specific fragility recurs across multiple independent sessions: the Kindle "daily" sales dashboard gives a tentative month-to-date figure finance can't rely on (~80K read live vs. ~75-77K in the final monthly report, FND-0049); at least once, Amazon silently revised a POD report already uploaded to NetSuite as final, after payment (~500 uploaded vs. ~490 paid), so finance now re-downloads it a second time post month-end (FND-0057); some ASINs in the Kindle report don't exist in SDNG's product records, patched by a manual workaround built by a developer no longer at Packt — a third key-person dependency alongside SACH (FND-0031) and the Keepa competitor-intelligence pipeline (FND-0013) (FND-0058); Amazon POD Japan arrives in JPY while Packt is paid in USD, so SDNG's daily feed is discarded for that channel and finance hand-converts using NetSuite's rate (FND-0059); and Amazon's account-to-region mapping under Packt's single account changed again in September, breaking UK/EU Kindle visibility the day before a discovery session (FND-0045) — the same instability class as FND-0057/0058/0059.

Elsewhere: trade invoices go straight into NetSuite and never reach SDNG, so trade revenue is entirely absent from BI reporting (FND-0061, REQ-0036 drafts the fix); the monthly direct-sales (Packt website) file carries stale "processing" order status that Stripe has already resolved, requiring a manual cross-check every time and feeding an unreconciled Power BI number — identified live in the session (by Arjun) as the actual cause of a recurring "the number is wrong" complaint (FND-0052, REQ-0029); a shipping charge on multi-title direct orders is duplicated per ISBN line rather than split ($110.95 recorded as $221.90, FND-0062); Academic B2B subscription revenue is allocated across titles by hand, per client, per currency, every month, entirely by one person (FND-0063, REQ-0037); events revenue is booked at a general level rather than per title because verticals lag on assigning a "search ID," an organisational gap not a technical one (FND-0064, REQ-0038); Coursera/Wiley/Sage "Expert Edge" title exports pay no author royalties at all under their revenue-share terms — the one royalty-free revenue category found so far (FND-0065); and royalties are calculated at title level rather than format level, so a wrong-format ISBN doesn't break attribution (FND-0066). Closing the books still costs a manual, minimum-two-hour monthly revenue-provisioning exercise because most channels report a month late (FND-0060), and how much shipping cost gets recovered from a trade customer is decided ad hoc, order by order, by an unregistered "Kajal's team" with no stated policy (FND-0051, Q-0026) [Source: packt-data-platform-v2 repo, ba-corpus/evidence/EV-0020,0021,0022,0023; findings FND-0048-0066 (selected) | 2026-09-08/10 | confidence: high].

## Events and Email Marketing Domains
**Events** (Vinit Somani, Head of Product, EV-0017): the same event can run simultaneously across up to three platforms (Eventbrite, Luma, AirMeet from launch) with no established identifier tying one attendee's activity together across them — email is the only proposed candidate, nothing built (FND-0041). All seven verticals' events revenue/performance sits on one shared, access-controlled-free spreadsheet that any team can corrupt (FND-0042). Eventbrite deducts VAT at source; Luma doesn't, leaving VAT reconciliation to Packt (FND-0043). Luma's own platform connection isn't working yet, unlike Eventbrite's flaky-but-functioning one (Q-0020).

**Email marketing** (Mrinmayee Kawalkar, Email Product Lead, EV-0024/0025): of ~800,000 users in the marketing database, only an estimated 300,000-400,000 have any behavioural data behind their segment — the rest are segmented by assumed taxonomy mapping, not observed behaviour (FND-0053). The current ESP, Omeda, syncs only monthly, which is named directly as the reason lifecycle-trigger emails can't run today; the Omeda contract runs to June 2027 (FND-0054). The weekly campaign-generation workflow (17 emails/week across 7 verticals) runs as an n8n automation on one person's own machine, not deployed anywhere shared (FND-0055). The title/taxonomy catalogue feeding those campaigns is rebuilt by hand every week from an Excel export that misses 10-15 actively bestselling titles most weeks — flagged directly by **Arjun James** in the session as "not the full data" (FND-0056) [Source: packt-data-platform-v2 repo, ba-corpus/evidence/EV-0017,EV-0024,EV-0025 | 2026-09-08/22 | confidence: high].

## Market Intelligence and Category Management (Kunal Chaudhari, Corpus Product)
Kunal Chaudhari's discovery session (EV-0018, 2026-09-09) is the corpus's second direct evidence source — after Arjun's own EV-0006 — naming Keepa and NPD/Circana BookScan explicitly (transcript corrections "Keeper"→Keepa and "book scan"→NPD/Circana BookScan, both at 13:31). He states plainly that once a commissioning pitch is approved, the market research behind it — BookScan, PD data, and the Keepa-derived sales-rank estimate alike — "is not getting captured anywhere" and is lost; the tool used for commissioning itself can't even be named on the recording ("Microsoft workflow, Microsoft something") (Q-0021). This is independent corroboration, from someone outside Arjun's own reporting line, that the Keepa-derived estimation work has real operational users beyond the team that built it — reinforcing FND-0013's framing of the pipeline as production-relevant.

Category management — shared tooling and best practice for market-opportunity discovery — is led by one person (ST-017, Kunal) supporting a workflow used across all 8 publishing category teams × 5 relationship leads = 40 people (FND-0046). ~30% of Amazon sales run through Amazon ads, which Packt has bought since 2018, but ad-performance data is visible only at campaign level, never aggregated globally, despite Amazon's own API exposing the data needed for a global view (FND-0047, REQ-0028 drafts the fix) — Q-0018 separately notes this ads data is distinct from, not a substitute for, the Keepa-derived sales-rank signal already in FND-0013/Q-0014, and that EV-0019's own 18-source signals register doesn't include Amazon Advertising among its scored candidates. The Amazon UK/EU account-mapping outage (FND-0045, above) surfaced in this same session [Source: packt-data-platform-v2 repo, ba-corpus/evidence/EV-0018 | 2026-09-09 | confidence: high].
